# New Zealand country guide

**Audience:** U.S. passport holders.  
**Facts checked:** 2026-11-05; individual dates appear with each fact.  
**Status:** Published facts, arranged in Japan’s ten-section guide format.  
**Formatted:** November 5, 2026.

Requirements can depend on trip purpose, length of stay, route, and personal circumstances. The conditions below identify facts that apply to a particular trip type. Regional, airport, and provider examples retain their stated scope. This collection does not provide a live forecast, exchange rate, or disruption feed.

## 1. Entry, passport, and stay type

- **Passport and permission checks** — Obtain the appropriate NZeTA or visa before travel. Carry a passport valid for three months after departure, with a blank entry-stamp page. Check separate permission for work or study.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — New Zealand: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/new-zealand.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 2. Getting there and border arrival

- **Submit the New Zealand Traveller Declaration** — Everyone entering New Zealand must complete a New Zealand Traveller Declaration. Use the official website or app and follow the submission timing for your flight or ship. Include the required travel, customs, and biosecurity information, and declare restricted goods honestly. Keep your passport handy at the border. The declaration is separate from an NZeTA or visa and does not replace either.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Immigration New Zealand — Arriving in New Zealand](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visit/what-you-need-to-visit-new-zealand/arriving-in-new-zealand/) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 3. Getting around the country

- **Check connections between towns and islands** — Depending on your route, getting around New Zealand may involve a car, local public transport, a domestic flight, or a long-distance coach. InterCity publishes coach routes and booking information for its network. Check the actual stop, travel date, luggage conditions, and any onward connection before booking. Local systems and regional services have their own tickets; do not assume that one booking covers the whole trip.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Immigration New Zealand — Getting around in New Zealand](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/getting-around-in-new-zealand/) (checked 2026-11-05); [InterCity — InterCity coach network and bookings](https://www.intercity.co.nz/) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 6. Health, care, and medicine

- **Health preparation for your itinerary** — Review routine protection and personal vaccine needs; use precautions appropriate to outdoor activities and the exact route. Review routine vaccines, including measles protection. Any global CDC notice is not by itself a country-specific outbreak report. Arrange advice before travel; this summary does not prescribe treatment.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention — New Zealand: Traveler View](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/new-zealand) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 7. Local culture and respectful travel

- **Wait for a welcome before entering a marae** — A marae is a Māori community meeting place, not an open-access visitor attraction. Do not walk onto one without being welcomed by the people of that place. If you are invited into the meeting house, remove your shoes, keep food and drink outside, and ask before taking photographs. Follow your hosts’ instructions because local customs can differ.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Tourism New Zealand — Marae: Māori meeting grounds](https://www.newzealand.com/int/feature/marae-maori-meeting-grounds/) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 8. Phones, internet, and staying connected

- **Activate a One NZ travel SIM after arrival** — One NZ offers Travel SIM and eSIM options for visitors. Its travel page says an eSIM can be bought online, with activation instructions to follow once you arrive in New Zealand. Check your unlocked phone’s compatibility and the current allowance, validity, calls, texts, and coverage before purchase. A New Zealand travel package is not automatically an overseas roaming package if you continue to another country.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [One New Zealand — Travel SIM and eSIM](https://one.nz/travel-sim/) (checked 2026-11-05); [One New Zealand — Travel SIM terms](https://one.nz/legal/terms-conditions/travel-sim/) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 9. Electricity, plugs, and device preparation

- **Plugs, supply, and device compatibility** — Electrical Safety First lists Type I plugs, 230 V, and 50 Hz. Check each device's input label and the actual outlet. A plug adapter changes fit, not voltage. A 120 V-only appliance needs manufacturer-approved conversion or should be left at home.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Electrical Safety First — New Zealand: travel adaptors](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/safety-advice/travel-advice/new-zealand/) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 10. Emergency help and U.S. consular support

- **Emergency help and U.S. assistance** — Within New Zealand, call 111 for an emergency. Non-emergency police assistance is available at 105. Other Pacific territories have different numbers. Local services handle immediate emergencies; use the linked U.S. country page for current consular contacts.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — New Zealand: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/new-zealand.html) (checked 2026-11-05).
