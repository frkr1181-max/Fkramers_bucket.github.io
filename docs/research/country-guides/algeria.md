# Algeria country guide

**Audience:** U.S. passport holders.

## 1. Entry, passport, and stay type

- **Passport and permission checks** — Use a passport with six months left after arrival and space for a stamp. Most visitors need a visa arranged before travel; limited approved southern tour arrangements have separate rules.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Algeria: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/algeria.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 2. Getting there and border arrival

- **Declare currency and keep your exchange documents** — Declare cash, bearer payment instruments, precious metals, and precious stones worth more than €1,000 (or equivalent) when entering or leaving Algeria. If you are a nonresident, keep the official exchange documents for amounts declared on entry; customs may ask for them when you leave. Use licensed exchange services and retain receipts with your travel documents.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Algerian Official Journal (Journal officiel) — Official Gazette: 2026 amendment to traveler currency declarations](https://www.joradp.dz/TRV/FLFin.pdf) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 3. Getting around the country

- **Plan rail trips through SNTF** — SNTF provides passenger rail services and publishes reservation, timetable, and fare information. Check the actual stations and service date before planning a connection, and buy through SNTF or an authorized seller. Rail does not serve every destination, so confirm the onward part of the journey separately. Keep regional security restrictions in mind when choosing a route.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Société Nationale des Transports Ferroviaires — Passenger train reservations](https://www.sntf.dz/index.php/reservation) (checked 2026-11-05); [Société Nationale des Transports Ferroviaires — Passenger station services](https://www.sntf.dz/index.php/en-gare) (checked 2026-11-05); [U.S. Department of State — Algeria: travel guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/algeria.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 4. Currency, payments, and customs cash declaration

- **Currency and payment planning** — Algerian dinar (DZD). Card use is limited outside major hotels; exchange money through authorized services and retain receipts.

## 5. Safety, weather, and disruptions

- **U.S. advisory and regional precautions** — The U.S. advisory is Level 2. Some border areas and remote Sahara routes have Level 4 warnings for terrorism and kidnapping. Read the regional restrictions before choosing a route. Advisory checked November 5, 2026; recheck before departure.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Algeria: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/algeria.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 6. Health, care, and medicine

- **Health preparation for your itinerary** — Discuss hepatitis A and polio protection with a clinician. CDC shows a global polio notice; check its current country-specific instructions. Review routine vaccines, including measles protection. Any global CDC notice is not by itself a country-specific outbreak report. Arrange advice before travel; this summary does not prescribe treatment.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention — Algeria: Traveler View](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/algeria) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 7. Local culture and respectful travel

- **Check before photographing official buildings** — Local law prohibits photographs of military and government buildings in Algeria. Do not assume that an ordinary-looking building is open for photography; some official buildings display the Algerian flag. If you are unsure, put the camera away and ask. Keep your passport or a copy available for identity checks. These are legal precautions, not a description of how every local person behaves.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Algeria: travel guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/algeria.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

Look at the current data allowance, validity, calls and texts, hotspot rules, and coverage for your route. Do not assume that a travel eSIM includes an Algerian phone number or reliable service in remote areas.

## 9. Electricity, plugs, and device preparation

- **Plugs, supply, and device compatibility** — Electrical Safety First lists Type C/F plugs, 230 V, and 50 Hz. Check each device's input label and the actual outlet. A plug adapter changes fit, not voltage. A 120 V-only appliance needs manufacturer-approved conversion or should be left at home.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Electrical Safety First — Algeria: travel adaptors](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/safety-advice/travel-advice/algeria/) (checked 2026-11-05).
