## 1. Entry, passport, and stay type

- **Passport and permission checks** — Use a passport valid for three months after departure with two blank pages. A 12-page U.S. emergency passport is not accepted for visa-free entry.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*
- **Short tourism within the Schengen limit** — U.S. tourists may usually visit Schengen for 90 days in 180, counting all member countries together. Earlier visits count. Employment and longer stays need separate permission.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.* **Applies to:** purpose: tourism; trip length: 1–90 days.

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Netherlands: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/netherlands.html) (checked 2026-11-05); [U.S. Department of State — U.S. Travelers in Europe](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/planning/guidance/europe.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 2. Getting there and border arrival

- **Use the correct customs channel at Schiphol** — At Amsterdam Schiphol, passport control and customs are separate checks. After collecting your bags, follow the green exit only if you have nothing to declare. If you have goods to declare or are unsure, use the red exit and ask a customs officer. Check restrictions before packing food, plants, medicines, or other controlled items. These airport steps describe Schiphol; other entry points may be laid out differently.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Royal Schiphol Group — When do I go through customs at Schiphol?](https://www.schiphol.nl/en/blog/when-do-i-go-through-customs-at-schiphol/) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 3. Getting around the country

- **Use the NS planner and check each connection** — The NS website and app help plan train journeys and connections by bus, tram, and metro. Check current service information and the ticket or payment method needed for each leg. A route shown in the planner is not a promise that every part uses the same fare. Follow the operator’s check-in and check-out instructions and keep the same payment method available throughout the journey.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Nederlandse Spoorwegen — How to travel by train](https://www.ns.nl/en/travel/how-to-travel-by-train) (checked 2026-11-05); [Nederlandse Spoorwegen — The NS app](https://www.ns.nl/en/travel/ns-app) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 7. Local culture and respectful travel

- **Leave room for cyclists in Amsterdam** — In Amsterdam, check for cycle lanes before crossing a street or stopping to look at a map. Keep walking and photo stops out of the bike path. If you rent a bicycle, learn the local signs and follow the lights and road rules rather than copying another rider. This is practical guidance for sharing Amsterdam’s streets; it does not mean cyclists always have right of way.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [I amsterdam, Amsterdam & Partners — Cycling in Amsterdam](https://www.iamsterdam.com/en/travel-stay/getting-around/cycling-in-amsterdam) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 9. Electricity, plugs, and device preparation

- **Bring an adapter that fits the Dutch supply** — The Netherlands uses Type C and F plugs with a 230 V, 50 Hz supply. Most U.S. plugs need an adapter, but an adapter does not change voltage or provide grounding by itself. Check each charger or appliance’s input label. Equipment marked for 100–240 V may work with the correct grounded connection; a 120 V-only appliance needs manufacturer-approved conversion or should be left at home.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Electrical Safety First — Netherlands travel adaptors](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/safety-advice/travel-advice/the-netherlands/) (checked 2026-11-05); [Netbeheer Nederland — Electricity supply quality](https://www.netbeheernederland.nl/veiligheid-en-infrastructuur/spanningskwaliteit) (checked 2026-11-05).
