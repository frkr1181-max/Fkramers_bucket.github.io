# Panama country guide

**Audience:** U.S. passport holders.  
**Facts checked:** 2026-11-05; individual dates appear with each fact.  
**Status:** Published facts, arranged in Japan’s ten-section guide format.  
**Formatted:** November 5, 2026.

Requirements can depend on trip purpose, length of stay, route, and personal circumstances. The conditions below identify facts that apply to a particular trip type. Regional, airport, and provider examples retain their stated scope. This collection does not provide a live forecast, exchange rate, or disruption feed.

## 1. Entry, passport, and stay type

- **Passport and permission checks** — Carry a passport with at least three months left after arrival. An onward or return ticket is required. Check the entry stamp and the stay authorized by immigration.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*
- **Short tourist stays** — The checked U.S. guidance describes visa-free tourist visits below 180 days. Confirm the exact allowance and departure date on your entry record with Panamanian immigration.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.* **Applies to:** purpose: tourism; trip length: 1–179 days.

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Panama: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/panama.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 2. Getting there and border arrival

- **Prepare for immigration and customs checks** — Use an official border crossing and have your passport, any required visa, and onward travel documents available. Panamanian immigration may interview you and inspect your documents or luggage. Complete the traveler declaration using the form or electronic process provided by Customs, and keep a copy of anything you declare. Follow the instructions at your actual arrival point rather than relying on an old form from another trip.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Panama National Migration Service — Tourist entry requirements](https://www.migracion.gob.pa/turistas/) (checked 2026-11-05); [Panama National Customs Authority — Traveler customs clearance](https://tramites.ana.gob.pa/w_proceso/GestiondeDespacho.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 3. Getting around the country

- **Travel between regions** — Panama's tourism authority describes domestic flights, roads, and buses. Albrook is a major bus hub. The Darién Gap prevents a continuous road journey to South America; check regional safety restrictions before choosing a route.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Panama Tourism Authority — Travel between regions](https://www.tourismpanama.com/travel-around-panama/) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 4. Currency, payments, and customs cash declaration

- **Currency and payment planning** — U.S. dollar (USD) and Panamanian balboa (PAB). Cards are common in cities; some rural businesses and resorts require cash.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Panama: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/panama.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 5. Safety, weather, and disruptions

- **U.S. advisory and regional precautions** — The U.S. advisory is Level 2. Some areas have Level 4 restrictions. Read the detailed Darién and other regional warnings rather than relying only on the national level. Advisory checked November 5, 2026; recheck before departure.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Panama: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/panama.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 6. Health, care, and medicine

- **Health preparation for your itinerary** — Malaria and yellow-fever recommendations vary by province and which side of the Canal Zone you visit. Ask a clinician to review the complete route. Review routine vaccines, including measles protection. Any global CDC notice is not by itself a country-specific outbreak report. Arrange advice before travel; this summary does not prescribe treatment.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention — Panama: Traveler View](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/panama) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 7. Local culture and respectful travel

- **Regional cultural diversity** — Panama's tourism authority describes Indigenous, African, and European influences across the country. Customs and visitor access differ by community and site; consult the local host or venue rather than assuming a single nationwide practice.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Panama Tourism Authority — Regional cultural diversity](https://www.tourismpanama.com/culture-cuisine/) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 8. Phones, internet, and staying connected

- **Tigo prepaid service information** — Tigo Panama publishes prepaid-service information. Ask the operator about visitor activation, identity checks, device compatibility, and current coverage and plan limits before buying; this fact does not promise nationwide reception.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Tigo Panama — Tigo prepaid service information](https://ayuda.tigo.com.pa/hc/centro-de-ayuda/articles/144175189814911-conoce-todo-sobre-el-servicio-prepago) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 9. Electricity, plugs, and device preparation

- **Plugs, supply, and device compatibility** — Electrical Safety First lists Type A/B plugs, 120 V, and 60 Hz. Check each device's input label and the actual outlet. A plug adapter changes fit, not voltage. A 120 V-only appliance needs manufacturer-approved conversion or should be left at home.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Electrical Safety First — Panama: travel adaptors](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/safety-advice/travel-advice/panama/) (checked 2026-11-05).
