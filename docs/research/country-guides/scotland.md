# Scotland country guide

**Audience:** U.S. passport holders.  
**Facts checked:** 2026-11-05; individual dates appear with each fact.  
**Status:** Published facts, arranged in Japan’s ten-section guide format.  
**Formatted:** November 5, 2026.

Requirements can depend on trip purpose, length of stay, route, and personal circumstances. The conditions below identify facts that apply to a particular trip type. Regional, airport, and provider examples retain their stated scope. This collection does not provide a live forecast, exchange rate, or disruption feed.

## 1. Entry, passport, and stay type

- **Passport and permission checks** — For travel to Scotland, carry a passport valid throughout the visit. UK entry rules apply: U.S. visitors normally need an ETA or the appropriate visa, with status-based exemptions.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*
- **UK visitor authorization** — A U.S. tourist normally needs a UK ETA before departure unless exempt. It supports visits of up to six months, not an automatic right to enter. For work or a different purpose, check the appropriate permission.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.* **Applies to:** purpose: tourism.

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Scotland: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/united-kingdom.html) (checked 2026-11-05); [UK Home Office — Get an electronic travel authorisation](https://www.gov.uk/eta) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 2. Getting there and border arrival

- **Edinburgh Airport tram connection** — Edinburgh Airport describes a tram connection to the city and Newhaven. Buy an appropriate ticket before boarding and check the live timetable. This applies to Edinburgh, not every Scottish airport.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Edinburgh Airport — Edinburgh Airport tram connection](https://www.edinburghairport.com/transport-links/trams) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 3. Getting around the country

- **Transport outside the main cities** — VisitScotland describes trains, buses, and ferries connecting cities, towns, and islands. Rural and remote services can be less frequent. Check connections and operator updates before travel.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [VisitScotland — Transport outside the main cities](https://www.visitscotland.com/travel-planning/scotland-faqs) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 7. Local culture and respectful travel

- **Tipping as a custom** — VisitScotland describes tipping for good restaurant or taxi service as a common choice, rather than a universal obligation. Check whether a service charge is already included. Venue arrangements vary.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [VisitScotland — Tipping as a custom](https://www.visitscotland.com/travel-planning/scotland-faqs) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 8. Phones, internet, and staying connected

- **Check internet access before travel** — VisitScotland says many accommodation providers offer Wi-Fi, but visitors should confirm before booking. Check the U.S. carrier's roaming terms and mobile coverage for the route; lodging Wi-Fi is not a promise of reception while traveling.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [VisitScotland — Check internet access before travel](https://www.visitscotland.com/travel-planning/scotland-faqs) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 9. Electricity, plugs, and device preparation

- **Plugs, supply, and device compatibility** — Electrical Safety First lists Type G plugs, 230 V, and 50 Hz. Check each device's input label and the actual outlet. A plug adapter changes fit, not voltage. A 120 V-only appliance needs manufacturer-approved conversion or should be left at home.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Electrical Safety First — Scotland: travel adaptors](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/safety-advice/travel-advice/united-kingdom/) (checked 2026-11-05).
