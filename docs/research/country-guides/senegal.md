## 1. Entry, passport, and stay type

- **Passport and permission checks** — Use a passport with six months left after arrival and space for a stamp. Check yellow-fever entry conditions for the countries visited or transited on the way.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*
- **Short tourist stays** — The checked U.S. guidance describes visa-free visits of up to 90 days. Check separate permission for work, study, residence, or a longer visit.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.* **Applies to:** purpose: tourism; trip length: 1–90 days.

## 6. Health, care, and medicine

- **Health preparation for your itinerary** — CDC recommends discussing malaria-prevention medicine for Senegal and yellow-fever vaccination for eligible travelers. Entry proof depends on the route; mosquito protection remains important. Review routine vaccines, including measles protection. Any global CDC notice is not by itself a country-specific outbreak report. Arrange advice before travel; this summary does not prescribe treatment.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention — Senegal: Traveler View](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/senegal) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 7. Local culture and respectful travel

- **Let your host guide a shared meal** — Teranga is a Wolof word for hospitality, and shared meals are one way it is expressed in Senegal. If you are invited to eat with others, let your host explain how the meal will be served rather than assuming a single rule for every family. Ceebu jën, a rice-and-fish dish associated with Saint-Louis, is one important food tradition; recipes and serving practices vary.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Embassy of Senegal in Qatar — Art and culture: Teranga](https://ambsenegal.qa/en/senegal/art-culture) (checked 2026-11-05); [UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage — Ceebu Jën, a culinary art of Senegal](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ceebu-jen-a-culinary-art-of-senegal-01748) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 9. Electricity, plugs, and device preparation

- **Plugs, supply, and device compatibility** — Electrical Safety First lists Type C/D/E/K plugs, 230 V, and 50 Hz. Check each device's input label and the actual outlet. A plug adapter changes fit, not voltage. A 120 V-only appliance needs manufacturer-approved conversion or should be left at home.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Electrical Safety First — Senegal: travel adaptors](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/safety-advice/travel-advice/senegal/) (checked 2026-11-05).
