# Germany country guide

**Audience:** U.S. passport holders.  
**Facts checked:** 2026-11-05; individual dates appear with each fact.  
**Status:** Published facts, arranged in Japan’s ten-section guide format.  
**Formatted:** November 5, 2026.

Requirements can depend on trip purpose, length of stay, route, and personal circumstances. The conditions below identify facts that apply to a particular trip type. Regional, airport, and provider examples retain their stated scope. This collection does not provide a live forecast, exchange rate, or disruption feed.

## 1. Entry, passport, and stay type

- **Passport and permission checks** — Carry a passport valid for three months after planned departure with two blank pages. Six months is recommended in U.S. guidance but is distinguished from the stated minimum.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*
- **Short tourism within the Schengen limit** — U.S. tourists may usually visit Schengen for 90 days in 180, counting all member countries together. Earlier visits count. Employment and longer stays need separate permission.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.* **Applies to:** purpose: tourism; trip length: 1–90 days.

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Germany: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/germany.html) (checked 2026-11-05); [U.S. Department of State — U.S. Travelers in Europe](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/planning/guidance/europe.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 2. Getting there and border arrival

- **Frankfurt Airport rail connections** — Frankfurt Airport has regional and long-distance rail connections. Follow the signs for your terminal and check the current railway timetable and ticket conditions. Other German airports have separate arrangements.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Frankfurt Airport — Frankfurt Airport rail connections](https://www.frankfurt-airport.com/en/transport-and-parking/to-from-the-airport/travel-by-train.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 3. Getting around the country

- **Use DB Navigator and check the ticket’s limits** — DB Navigator provides journey planning, current travel information, and tickets for supported rail and local transport services. Use the official Deutsche Bahn download links and check your route before boarding. A ticket for regional or local transport does not automatically cover a long-distance train. Look at the trains, operators, and reservation conditions included in the fare you choose.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Deutsche Bahn — DB Navigator](https://int.bahn.de/en/booking-information/db-navigator) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 4. Currency, payments, and customs cash declaration

- **Currency and payment planning** — Germany uses the euro (EUR). Check a dated USD/EUR quote and your payment provider's fees before exchanging.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [European Central Bank — Our money: euro-area membership](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/euro/intro/html/index.en.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 5. Safety, weather, and disruptions

- **U.S. advisory and regional precautions** — The U.S. advisory is Level 2 because of terrorism. Review current official alerts and remain alert in busy public places. Advisory checked November 5, 2026; recheck before departure.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Germany: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/germany.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 6. Health, care, and medicine

- **Health preparation for your itinerary** — Discuss tick-borne encephalitis protection for substantial outdoor exposure in affected areas. CDC also shows a global polio notice; check current instructions. Review routine vaccines, including measles protection. Any global CDC notice is not by itself a country-specific outbreak report. Arrange advice before travel; this summary does not prescribe treatment.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention — Germany: Traveler View](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/germany) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 7. Local culture and respectful travel

- **Tipping is a choice for service** — Tipping is voluntary in Germany. Berlin’s visitor guidance describes it as a way to show satisfaction with service, not a compulsory extra charge. Check the bill and decide what you want to leave. This is a service custom; it does not mean every café, taxi, or hotel expects the same amount or handles tips in the same way.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [City of Berlin — Tipping etiquette](https://www.berlin.de/en/tourism/travel-information/1758234-2862820-tipping.en.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 8. Phones, internet, and staying connected

- **Telekom prepaid eSIM information** — Telekom describes eSIM options for prepaid as well as contract plans. Before buying, confirm visitor identification requirements, device compatibility, activation, coverage, and the selected plan's limits directly with the operator.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Deutsche Telekom — Telekom prepaid eSIM information](https://www.telekom.de/shop/mobilfunk/esim) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 9. Electricity, plugs, and device preparation

- **Plugs, supply, and device compatibility** — Electrical Safety First lists Type C/F plugs, 230 V, and 50 Hz. Check each device's input label and the actual outlet. A plug adapter changes fit, not voltage. A 120 V-only appliance needs manufacturer-approved conversion or should be left at home.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Electrical Safety First — Germany: travel adaptors](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/safety-advice/travel-advice/germany/) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 10. Emergency help and U.S. consular support

- **Emergency help and U.S. assistance** — For an ambulance, call 112. For police, call 110. Local services handle immediate emergencies; use the linked U.S. country page for current consular contacts.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Germany: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/germany.html) (checked 2026-11-05).
