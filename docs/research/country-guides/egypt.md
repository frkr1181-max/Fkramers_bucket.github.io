# Egypt country guide

**Audience:** U.S. passport holders.  
**Facts checked:** 2026-11-05; individual dates appear with each fact.  
**Status:** Published facts, arranged in Japan’s ten-section guide format.  
**Formatted:** November 5, 2026.

Requirements can depend on trip purpose, length of stay, route, and personal circumstances. The conditions below identify facts that apply to a particular trip type. Regional, airport, and provider examples retain their stated scope. This collection does not provide a live forecast, exchange rate, or disruption feed.

## 1. Entry, passport, and stay type

- **Passport and permission checks** — A U.S. traveler needs a visa. Carry a passport with six months of validity and a blank page. The State Department describes 30-day tourist visas on arrival; confirm the appropriate route before departure.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Egypt: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/egypt.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 2. Getting there and border arrival

- **Have your e-Visa paperwork ready** — If you enter Egypt with an e-Visa, print the approved visa and keep it with your passport. Have your travel itinerary and accommodation details available. The e-Visa portal also lists supporting letters for business or family visits. Approval of the e-Visa does not guarantee admission, so answer the arrival officer’s questions and carry the documents that support the purpose of your visit.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Egyptian Ministry of Interior e-Visa Portal — Egypt e-Visa: arrival requirements](https://visa2egypt.gov.eg/eVisa/FAQ) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 3. Getting around the country

- **Rail and bus travel examples** — Egypt's tourism authority describes rail links serving the Nile corridor and bus services in several regions. Confirm the actual route, current operation, and safety restrictions with the operator; coverage differs by region.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Egyptian Tourism Authority — Rail and bus travel examples](https://www.experienceegypt.eg/en/usefulinfo) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 4. Currency, payments, and customs cash declaration

- **Currency and payment planning** — Egyptian pound (EGP). The country guidance identifies ATMs as one way to obtain local currency. Confirm the quoted currency before paying.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Egypt: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/egypt.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 5. Safety, weather, and disruptions

- **U.S. advisory and regional precautions** — The U.S. advisory is Level 2. Northern and Middle Sinai, border military zones, and Western Desert travel outside licensed tours have stronger restrictions. Read the regional warnings. Advisory checked November 5, 2026; recheck before departure.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Egypt: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/egypt.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 6. Health, care, and medicine

- **Health preparation for your itinerary** — Discuss hepatitis A and food/water precautions. Yellow-fever entry proof can depend on travel from other countries, even when direct U.S. arrivals do not need it. Review routine vaccines, including measles protection. Any global CDC notice is not by itself a country-specific outbreak report. Arrange advice before travel; this summary does not prescribe treatment.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention — Egypt: Traveler View](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/egypt) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 7. Local culture and respectful travel

- **Dress at places of worship** — Egypt's tourism authority advises modest clothing for visits to mosques, churches, and other places of worship, including covered shoulders and legs. Ask the venue about head coverings and other entry rules. This is venue guidance.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Egyptian Tourism Authority — Dress at places of worship](https://www.experienceegypt.eg/en/usefulinfo) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 8. Phones, internet, and staying connected

- **Ask about Vodafone’s tourist line before buying** — Vodafone Egypt advertises a Tourist Line for visitors. Use an official shop to check the current package and the documents needed to activate it with your passport. Confirm whether the offer is a physical SIM or an eSIM, whether your phone is compatible, and what happens if your visit is extended. Ask separately about calls to the United States, data limits, and coverage; an advertised tourist offer does not confirm every device or visitor’s eligibility.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Vodafone Egypt — Tourist Line](https://web.vodafone.com.eg//en/tourist-line) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 9. Electricity, plugs, and device preparation

- **Plugs, supply, and device compatibility** — Electrical Safety First lists Type C/F plugs, 220 V, and 50 Hz. Check each device's input label and the actual outlet. A plug adapter changes fit, not voltage. A 120 V-only appliance needs manufacturer-approved conversion or should be left at home.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Electrical Safety First — Egypt: travel adaptors](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/safety-advice/travel-advice/egypt/) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 10. Emergency help and U.S. consular support

- **Emergency help and U.S. assistance** — For an ambulance, call 123. For U.S. consular help after contacting local responders, the Embassy Cairo number is +20-2-2797-3300. Local services handle immediate emergencies; use the linked U.S. country page for current consular contacts.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Egypt: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/egypt.html) (checked 2026-11-05).
