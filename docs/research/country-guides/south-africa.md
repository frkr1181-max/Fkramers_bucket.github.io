# South Africa country guide

**Audience:** U.S. passport holders.  
**Facts checked:** 2026-11-05; individual dates appear with each fact.  
**Status:** Published facts, arranged in Japan’s ten-section guide format.  
**Formatted:** November 5, 2026.

Requirements can depend on trip purpose, length of stay, route, and personal circumstances. The conditions below identify facts that apply to a particular trip type. Regional, airport, and provider examples retain their stated scope. This collection does not provide a live forecast, exchange rate, or disruption feed.

## 1. Entry, passport, and stay type

- **Passport and permission checks** — Use a passport valid for at least 30 days beyond departure, with two consecutive blank visa pages for each entry. Review route-specific yellow-fever rules and any requirements for children.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*
- **Short tourist stays** — The checked U.S. country guidance describes visa-free short tourist visits below 90 days. Confirm the granted stay at entry; work and longer stays require separate permission.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.* **Applies to:** purpose: tourism; trip length: 1–89 days.

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — South Africa: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/south-africa.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 2. Getting there and border arrival

- **O.R. Tambo ground transport** — At Johannesburg's O.R. Tambo airport, the airport lists Gautrain, authorized taxis, and shuttles. Use the airport information desk to identify authorized services; other airports have different arrangements.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Airports Company South Africa — O.R. Tambo ground transport](https://www.airports.co.za/airports/or-tambo-international-airport/transport/public-transport) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 3. Getting around the country

- **Gautrain trip planning** — Gautrain provides a regional journey planner, schedules, fares, and bus information for its network. Check current services and connections. It is a regional system, not a nationwide rail pass.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Gautrain — Gautrain trip planning](https://www.gautrain.co.za/) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 4. Currency, payments, and customs cash declaration

- **Currency and payment planning** — South Africa uses the rand (ZAR). Check a dated USD/ZAR quote and your payment provider's charges; a reference rate is not a guaranteed transaction rate.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [South African Reserve Bank — Banknotes and coin](https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/what-we-do/banknotes-and-coin/Banknotes) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 5. Safety, weather, and disruptions

- **U.S. advisory and regional precautions** — The U.S. advisory is Level 2 for crime, terrorism, unrest, and kidnapping. Violent crime is a significant concern; avoid walking alone after dark and read regional restrictions. Advisory checked November 5, 2026; recheck before departure.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — South Africa: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/south-africa.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 6. Health, care, and medicine

- **Health preparation for your itinerary** — Malaria advice depends on the region, including some northeastern border areas. Discuss the route and seasonal exposure with a clinician; yellow-fever entry proof can depend on prior travel. Review routine vaccines, including measles protection. Any global CDC notice is not by itself a country-specific outbreak report. Arrange advice before travel; this summary does not prescribe treatment.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention — South Africa: Traveler View](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/south-africa) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 7. Local culture and respectful travel

- **Expect different languages across communities** — South Africa has 12 official languages, including South African Sign Language. Do not assume that everyone has the same first language or prefers to communicate in English. Ask what works for the person helping you, and keep an address or place name written down if pronunciation is getting in the way. Language and cultural practices vary by community; one city’s habits do not describe the whole country.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [South African Government — People of South Africa](https://www.gov.za/about-sa/south-africas-people) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 8. Phones, internet, and staying connected

- **SIM registration and identity checks** — South Africa's Justice Department describes RICA SIM-registration requirements and identity-verification concerns, including for foreign nationals. Ask the licensed operator for current visitor registration documents and avoid pre-registered cards from unknown sellers.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [South Africa Department of Justice and Constitutional Development — SIM registration and identity checks](https://www.justice.gov.za/m_statements/2026/20260326-RICA.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 9. Electricity, plugs, and device preparation

- **Plugs, supply, and device compatibility** — Electrical Safety First lists Type C/M/N plugs, 230 V, and 50 Hz. Check each device's input label and the actual outlet. A plug adapter changes fit, not voltage. A 120 V-only appliance needs manufacturer-approved conversion or should be left at home.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Electrical Safety First — South Africa: travel adaptors](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/safety-advice/travel-advice/south-africa/) (checked 2026-11-05).
