# Mexico country guide

**Audience:** U.S. passport holders.  
**Facts checked:** 2026-11-05; individual dates appear with each fact.  
**Status:** Published facts, arranged in Japan’s ten-section guide format.  
**Formatted:** November 5, 2026.

Requirements can depend on trip purpose, length of stay, route, and personal circumstances. The conditions below identify facts that apply to a particular trip type. Regional, airport, and provider examples retain their stated scope. This collection does not provide a live forecast, exchange rate, or disruption feed.

## 1. Entry, passport, and stay type

- **Passport and permission checks** — Carry a valid U.S. passport. Confirm the period granted at entry; a visitor allowance is not permission to work or a guaranteed stay. Work, residence, and longer stays need the appropriate authorization.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*
- **Short tourist stays** — U.S. tourists generally use the visitor route for visits of up to 180 days. The immigration officer determines the period actually granted; 180 days is not automatic. Employment is outside this allowance.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.* **Applies to:** purpose: tourism; trip length: 1–180 days.

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Mexico: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/mexico.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 2. Getting there and border arrival

- **Keep a copy of the stay you are granted** — At Mexican immigration, be ready to explain your visit and show your accommodation and return or onward plans. Check the departure date or length of stay you are actually granted; the maximum visitor allowance is not automatic. Keep your entry record with your travel documents. The process can differ between an airport, an automated gate, and a land border.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Mexican Government / National Institute of Migration — Requirements at the Mexican point of entry](https://www.gob.mx/cms/uploads/attachment/file/807109/Formato_requisitos_ingreso_a_M_xico_2023_ES.pdf) (checked 2026-11-05); [U.S. Department of State — Mexico: travel guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/mexico.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 3. Getting around the country

- **Check state restrictions before choosing a route** — Plan intercity travel for daylight and check the restrictions for every Mexican state on your route. For local rides, arrange a vehicle through a regulated taxi stand or an app-based service where it operates, rather than flagging down a street taxi. Keep the pickup and destination clear before you leave. Availability of a route or ride in an app is not a guarantee that it is safe.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Mexico: travel guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/mexico.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 4. Currency, payments, and customs cash declaration

- **Currency and payment planning** — Mexican peso (MXN). Cards are widely used, but markets may require cash. A $ sign can mean pesos; check the currency and ATM charges.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Mexico: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/mexico.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 5. Safety, weather, and disruptions

- **U.S. advisory and regional precautions** — The national U.S. advisory is Level 2, with state-specific Levels 1 through 4. Check every state on the route and follow the published travel restrictions. Avoid intercity night travel. Advisory checked November 5, 2026; recheck before departure.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Mexico: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/mexico.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 6. Health, care, and medicine

- **Health preparation for your itinerary** — Malaria prevention depends on the region. CDC also lists a northern-Mexico Rocky Mountain spotted fever notice. Discuss the exact route and protect against mosquito and tick bites. Review routine vaccines, including measles protection. Any global CDC notice is not by itself a country-specific outbreak report. Arrange advice before travel; this summary does not prescribe treatment.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention — Mexico: Traveler View](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/mexico) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 7. Local culture and respectful travel

- **Help protect archaeological sites** — At Mexican archaeological sites, follow the marked visitor routes and the rules for the specific monument. INAH’s visitor guidance asks people not to lean on or touch historic walls. Treat barriers and closed areas as protection for the site, not as an invitation to find another way in. Check the site’s own photography rules before using a flash, tripod, or other equipment.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Mexico National Institute of Anthropology and History — Guide to visiting an archaeological site](https://conservacion.inah.gob.mx/pdf/tu_conservas/conservacion_preventiva.pdf) (checked 2026-11-05); [Mexico National Institute of Anthropology and History — Archaeological sites directory](https://www.inah.gob.mx/zonas-arqueologicas) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 8. Phones, internet, and staying connected

- **Prepare for Telcel tourist eSIM registration** — Telcel offers a Tourist International eSIM for unlocked phones that support its network and eSIM technology. You need an internet connection for installation. Its registration instructions ask for a valid passport and a live identity check using the phone’s camera. Follow the official purchase and activation steps, and check your selected plan’s destinations, data, calling, and validity limits. Do not assume that every Telcel package has the same cross-border coverage.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Telcel — Tourist International eSIM](https://www.telcel.com/travel-to-mexico/esim-english) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 9. Electricity, plugs, and device preparation

- **Plugs, supply, and device compatibility** — Electrical Safety First lists Type A/B plugs, 120 V, and 60 Hz. Check each device's input label and the actual outlet. A plug adapter changes fit, not voltage. A 120 V-only appliance needs manufacturer-approved conversion or should be left at home.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Electrical Safety First — Mexico: travel adaptors](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/safety-advice/travel-advice/mexico/) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 10. Emergency help and U.S. consular support

- **Emergency help and U.S. assistance** — Call 911 for emergencies. English is not available from every operator; seek Spanish-language help if needed. Local services handle immediate emergencies; use the linked U.S. country page for current consular contacts.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Mexico: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/mexico.html) (checked 2026-11-05).
