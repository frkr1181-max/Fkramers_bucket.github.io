**Audience:** U.S. passport holders.  
**Facts checked:** 2026-11-05; individual dates appear with each fact.  
**Status:** Published facts, arranged in Japan’s ten-section guide format.  
**Formatted:** November 5, 2026.

Requirements can depend on trip purpose, length of stay, route, and personal circumstances. The conditions below identify facts that apply to a particular trip type. Regional, airport, and provider examples retain their stated scope. This collection does not provide a live forecast, exchange rate, or disruption feed.

## 1. Entry, passport, and stay type

- **Passport and permission checks** — Carry a passport valid for three months beyond Schengen departure and a blank page. France does not accept the 12-page U.S. emergency passport for entry.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*
- **Short tourism within the Schengen limit** — U.S. tourists may usually visit Schengen for 90 days in 180, counting all member countries together. Earlier visits count. Employment and longer stays need separate permission.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.* **Applies to:** purpose: tourism; trip length: 1–90 days.

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — France: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/france.html) (checked 2026-11-05); [U.S. Department of State — U.S. Travelers in Europe](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/planning/guidance/europe.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 3. Getting around the country

- **Plan train journeys with SNCF Connect** — SNCF Connect can help you plan and book French rail journeys and some connecting transport. Its official iOS and Android apps include services such as TGV INOUI, TER, and INTERCITÉS, with other operators and local networks offered where supported. Check whether the ticket covers the whole route, any seat reservation, and the change or refund conditions. Local transport tickets and long-distance train tickets are not automatically interchangeable.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [SNCF Connect — TER regional train planning and tickets](https://www.sncf-connect.com/en-en/ter) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 6. Health, care, and medicine

- **Health preparation for your itinerary** — Prevent tick and other insect bites during outdoor activities. Ask a clinician about advice for the exact itinerary and personal health. Review routine vaccines, including measles protection. Any global CDC notice is not by itself a country-specific outbreak report. Arrange advice before travel; this summary does not prescribe treatment.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention — France: Traveler View](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/france) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 7. Local culture and respectful travel

- **Know the difference between service and a tip** — In France, a tip is optional. Where a restaurant or bar charges for service, that charge is included in the bill and the displayed information must identify it. Check the bill before adding anything extra. A voluntary tip is separate from the service charge, so you do not need to treat a U.S.-style tip as an automatic additional cost.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [French Ministry of the Economy / DGCCRF — Pourboire: tips and service charges](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/dgccrf/les-fiches-pratiques/pourboire) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 9. Electricity, plugs, and device preparation

- **Plugs, supply, and device compatibility** — Electrical Safety First lists Type C/E plugs, 230 V, and 50 Hz. Check each device's input label and the actual outlet. A plug adapter changes fit, not voltage. A 120 V-only appliance needs manufacturer-approved conversion or should be left at home.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Electrical Safety First — France: travel adaptors](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/safety-advice/travel-advice/france/) (checked 2026-11-05).
