# Argentina country guide

**Audience:** U.S. passport holders.  
**Facts checked:** 2026-11-05; individual dates appear with each fact.  
**Status:** Published facts, arranged in Japan’s ten-section guide format.  
**Formatted:** November 5, 2026.

Requirements can depend on trip purpose, length of stay, route, and personal circumstances. The conditions below identify facts that apply to a particular trip type. Regional, airport, and provider examples retain their stated scope. This collection does not provide a live forecast, exchange rate, or disruption feed.

## 1. Entry, passport, and stay type

- **Passport and permission checks** — An ordinary U.S. passport must be valid on arrival and have room for an entry stamp. Check separate permission for work, study, or stays beyond the visitor allowance.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*
- **Short tourist stays** — For an ordinary U.S. passport, tourist visits of up to 90 days do not require a visa. This does not authorize employment or settle requirements for other purposes.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.* **Applies to:** purpose: tourism; trip length: 1–90 days.

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Argentina: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/argentina.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 2. Getting there and border arrival

- **Check your baggage declaration before arrival** — Argentina requires a customs declaration in specific cases, including goods over the allowance, items controlled by SENASA, and cash or monetary instruments worth US$10,000 or more. Check your bags before you travel and follow the declaration instructions for your arrival point. Do not assume that food, plants, or animal products can enter just because they are for personal use.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [ARCA, Argentine Revenue and Customs Control Agency — Travelers: baggage rules](https://arca.gob.ar/viajeros/ayuda/regimen-equipaje.asp) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 3. Getting around the country

- **Check the rail operator for each route** — Argentina has metropolitan and long-distance passenger rail services, but they are not one continuous network serving every destination. Use the official passenger-line directory to identify the operator and check current routes, tickets, and service notices. Buenos Aires commuter lines are a regional system; do not assume their ticket or payment arrangements cover a long-distance trip elsewhere in the country.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Argentina National Transport Regulation Commission — Passenger railway lines and operators](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/transporte/cnrt/lineas-de-trenes-pasajeros) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 4. Currency, payments, and customs cash declaration

- **Currency and payment planning** — Argentine peso (ARS). Cards and ATMs are available, but some U.S. bank cards fail at particular ATMs; allow for withdrawal limits.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

## 5. Safety, weather, and disruptions

- **U.S. advisory and regional precautions** — The U.S. advisory is Level 1. Protect belongings in busy places; Rosario has additional violence risks. Read current local warnings before travel. Advisory checked November 5, 2026; recheck before departure.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Argentina: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/argentina.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 6. Health, care, and medicine

- **Health preparation for your itinerary** — Discuss hepatitis A and itinerary-specific yellow-fever advice. CDC recommends yellow-fever vaccine for eligible travelers to Corrientes and Misiones; other routes differ. Review routine vaccines, including measles protection. Any global CDC notice is not by itself a country-specific outbreak report. Arrange advice before travel; this summary does not prescribe treatment.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention — Argentina: Traveler View](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/argentina) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 8. Phones, internet, and staying connected

- **Take your passport when arranging a Personal tourist line** — Personal offers prepaid lines for visitors to Argentina. Its tourist page directs people who bought a SIM from a kiosk to an official service point for activation and asks them to have ID or a passport ready. For an unlocked, compatible phone, ask Personal about the eSIM option as well. Confirm the package, validity, coverage, and calling limits before paying; a SIM bought at a kiosk may not already be active.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Telecom Argentina / Personal — Personal prepaid SIM for tourists](https://www.personal.com.ar/prepago/turista) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 9. Electricity, plugs, and device preparation

- **Plugs, supply, and device compatibility** — Electrical Safety First lists Type I plugs, 220 V, and 50 Hz. Check each device's input label and the actual outlet. A plug adapter changes fit, not voltage. A 120 V-only appliance needs manufacturer-approved conversion or should be left at home.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Electrical Safety First — Argentina: travel adaptors](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/safety-advice/travel-advice/argentina/) (checked 2026-11-05).
