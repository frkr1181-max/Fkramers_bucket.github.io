## 1. Entry, passport, and stay type

- **Passport and permission checks** — Carry a passport valid for three months after departure with two blank pages. A 12-page U.S. emergency passport does not qualify for visa-free entry.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*
- **Short tourism within the Schengen limit** — U.S. tourists may usually visit Schengen for 90 days in 180, counting all member countries together. Earlier visits count. Employment and longer stays need separate permission.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.* **Applies to:** purpose: tourism; trip length: 1–90 days.

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Norway: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/norway.html) (checked 2026-11-05); [U.S. Department of State — U.S. Travelers in Europe](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/planning/guidance/europe.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 2. Getting there and border arrival

- **Ask customs if you are unsure what to declare** — At Norwegian border crossings with red and green channels, use red for goods that need duty, tax, or a special permit. Use green only when your goods meet the allowance and other conditions. Walking through green is itself a declaration that you have nothing requiring customs clearance. If you are unsure, take the red channel and ask before leaving the customs area.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Norwegian Customs — Red or green channel](https://www.toll.no/en/shopping-abroad/red-or-green-channel/) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 6. Health, care, and medicine

- **Health preparation for your itinerary** — Discuss tick-borne encephalitis protection when outdoor exposure in affected areas is substantial. Review routine vaccines, including measles protection. Any global CDC notice is not by itself a country-specific outbreak report. Arrange advice before travel; this summary does not prescribe treatment.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention — Norway: Traveler View](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/norway) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 7. Local culture and respectful travel

- **Respectful outdoor access** — Norway's right to roam has conditions. It applies to open, uncultivated land rather than every private garden or farmed area. Respect wildlife, local communities, and posted restrictions; check the official access guidance before camping.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Visit Norway — Respectful outdoor access](https://www.visitnorway.com/plan-your-trip/visitor-guidelines/) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 8. Phones, internet, and staying connected

- **Use a Telia shop if you do not have Norwegian digital ID** — Telia sells prepaid starter SIMs in Norway. Its online process uses BankID, but people without BankID or a Norwegian personal number can buy and register through a Telia shop. Ask which identity documents the shop needs before you go. Prepaid eSIM activation has extra ownership and BankID requirements, so confirm that route with customer service rather than assuming it works like the physical SIM. Check the current bundle and coverage for your trip.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Telia Norway — Telia Kontant prepaid service](https://www.telia.no/mobilabonnement/kontantkort/) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 9. Electricity, plugs, and device preparation

- **Plugs, supply, and device compatibility** — Electrical Safety First lists Type C/F plugs, 230 V, and 50 Hz. Check each device's input label and the actual outlet. A plug adapter changes fit, not voltage. A 120 V-only appliance needs manufacturer-approved conversion or should be left at home.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Electrical Safety First — Norway: travel adaptors](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/safety-advice/travel-advice/norway/) (checked 2026-11-05).
