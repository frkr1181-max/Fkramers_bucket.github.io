# Iraq country guide

**Audience:** U.S. passport holders.  
**Facts checked:** 2026-11-05; individual dates appear with each fact.  
**Status:** Published facts, arranged in Japan’s ten-section guide format.  
**Formatted:** November 5, 2026.

Requirements can depend on trip purpose, length of stay, route, and personal circumstances. The conditions below identify facts that apply to a particular trip type. Regional, airport, and provider examples retain their stated scope. This collection does not provide a live forecast, exchange rate, or disruption feed.

## 1. Entry, passport, and stay type

- **Passport and permission checks** — Arrange the required Iraqi visa before departure through the official e-Visa route. Carry a passport valid for six months after arrival with space for a stamp. Entry permission does not remove the travel risks.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Iraq: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/iraq.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 2. Getting there and border arrival

- **Check the entry route before travel** — The U.S. advisory is Level 4: do not travel to Iraq. If you are already arranging essential travel, confirm the entry point and visa route before departure. Federal Iraq and the Iraqi Kurdistan Region have different entry arrangements; do not assume that one region’s permission covers the other. Use the official visa instructions and confirm your documents with the responsible authority and airline.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Iraq: travel guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/iraq.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 3. Getting around the country

- **Do not treat ordinary transport options as safe travel clearance** — The U.S. advisory is Level 4: do not travel to Iraq. Current U.S. guidance does not recommend taxis, ride-share services, or public transport other than civil aviation for U.S. travelers. Road security and service conditions can change quickly. If you are already in Iraq, follow current security instructions and review departure options rather than relying on a routine tourist route or app.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Iraq: travel guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/iraq.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 4. Currency, payments, and customs cash declaration

- **Currency and payment planning** — Iraqi dinar (IQD). Cash is important; ATMs can be unreliable or unavailable outside major cities, and card use may attract extra charges.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Iraq: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/iraq.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 5. Safety, weather, and disruptions

- **U.S. advisory and regional precautions** — The U.S. advisory is Level 4: do not travel. Terrorism, kidnapping, armed conflict, crime, and limited emergency assistance create serious risks. Read the full advisory before making plans. Advisory checked November 5, 2026; recheck before departure.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Iraq: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/iraq.html) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 6. Health, care, and medicine

- **Health preparation for your itinerary** — Discuss hepatitis A and other itinerary-specific vaccines and food/water precautions with a clinician. Medical preparation does not remove the Level 4 travel warning. Review routine vaccines, including measles protection. Any global CDC notice is not by itself a country-specific outbreak report. Arrange advice before travel; this summary does not prescribe treatment.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention — Iraq: Traveler View](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/iraq) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 7. Local culture and respectful travel

- **Respect local practices during Ramadan** — The U.S. advises against travel to Iraq. If you are already there, be aware that Ramadan is observed and local practices can affect daily routines. Check local rules and opening arrangements before making plans, and follow your host’s guidance around religious observance. Ramadan follows the lunar calendar, so do not reuse dates from a previous year. This does not remove the wider security risks.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Iraq: travel guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/iraq.html) (checked 2026-11-05); [Australian Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade — Iraq: local customs](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/middle-east/iraq) (checked 2026-11-05); [Australian Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade — Travelling during Ramadan](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/news-and-updates/travelling-during-ramadan) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 8. Phones, internet, and staying connected

- **Check the limits of a visitor mobile line** — If you are already in Iraq, Asiacell offers a Hala Line for visitors with a stated 30-day line validity. Confirm activation documents, phone compatibility, coverage, and the package’s data allowance directly with the operator. Some international-call bundles name specific countries, so do not assume they include calls to the United States. Mobile service does not remove the security risks or guarantee that help is reachable during disruption.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

## 9. Electricity, plugs, and device preparation

- **Plugs, supply, and device compatibility** — Electrical Safety First lists Type C/D/G plugs, 230 V, and 50 Hz. Check each device's input label and the actual outlet. A plug adapter changes fit, not voltage. A 120 V-only appliance needs manufacturer-approved conversion or should be left at home.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [Electrical Safety First — Iraq: travel adaptors](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/safety-advice/travel-advice/iraq/) (checked 2026-11-05).

## 10. Emergency help and U.S. consular support

- **Emergency help and U.S. assistance** — U.S. country guidance lists 911 for general emergencies, 104 for police, and 122 for ambulance. Emergency help may be limited. Local services handle immediate emergencies; use the linked U.S. country page for current consular contacts.  
  *Checked: 2026-11-05.*

- Sources: [U.S. Department of State — Iraq: travel advisory and country guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/iraq.html) (checked 2026-11-05).
