### Plugs & voltage

**Plugs, supply, and device compatibility** — Electrical Safety First lists Type C/D/G plugs, 230 V, and 50 Hz. Check each device's input label and the actual outlet. A plug adapter changes fit, not voltage. A 120 V-only appliance needs manufacturer-approved conversion or should be left at home.

Checked: 2026-11-05.

Original section sources: [Electrical Safety First — Iraq: travel adaptors](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/safety-advice/travel-advice/iraq/) (checked 2026-11-05)

### Vaccines

**Health preparation for your itinerary** — Discuss hepatitis A and other itinerary-specific vaccines and food/water precautions with a clinician. Medical preparation does not remove the Level 4 travel warning. Review routine vaccines, including measles protection. Any global CDC notice is not by itself a country-specific outbreak report. Arrange advice before travel; this summary does not prescribe treatment.

### Arrival steps

**Check the entry route before travel** — The U.S. advisory is Level 4: do not travel to Iraq. If you are already arranging essential travel, confirm the entry point and visa route before departure. Federal Iraq and the Iraqi Kurdistan Region have different entry arrangements; do not assume that one region’s permission covers the other. Use the official visa instructions and confirm your documents with the responsible authority and airline.

### Taxis

**Do not treat ordinary transport options as safe travel clearance** — The U.S. advisory is Level 4: do not travel to Iraq. Current U.S. guidance does not recommend taxis, ride-share services, or public transport other than civil aviation for U.S. travelers. Road security and service conditions can change quickly. If you are already in Iraq, follow current security instructions and review departure options rather than relying on a routine tourist route or app.

## Local culture

### Etiquette

**Respect local practices during Ramadan** — The U.S. advises against travel to Iraq. If you are already there, be aware that Ramadan is observed and local practices can affect daily routines. Check local rules and opening arrangements before making plans, and follow your host’s guidance around religious observance. Ramadan follows the lunar calendar, so do not reuse dates from a previous year. This does not remove the wider security risks.

Checked: 2026-11-05.

Original section sources: [U.S. Department of State — Iraq: travel guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/iraq.html) (checked 2026-11-05); [Australian Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade — Iraq: local customs](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/middle-east/iraq) (checked 2026-11-05); [Australian Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade — Travelling during Ramadan](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/news-and-updates/travelling-during-ramadan) (checked 2026-11-05)
