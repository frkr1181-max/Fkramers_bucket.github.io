### Arrival forms

**Complete the passenger declaration and declare restricted items** — Have your passport and the passenger declaration required for your flight ready at arrival. Most travelers currently use an Incoming Passenger Card; a digital Australia Travel Declaration is available only on eligible routes and flights. Declare the food, plant, animal, and other items covered by the questions. If you are unsure about something in your bag, declare it and ask a border officer.

## Phones & tech

### eSIM

**Check Telstra’s visitor prepaid options** — Telstra offers prepaid service for overseas visitors, with physical SIM and eSIM options on supported plans. Check your phone’s compatibility, the activation and identity requirements, and coverage for the places you will visit. Compare the full price and renewal settings with your U.S. roaming plan. ### Plugs & voltage

**Plugs, supply, and device compatibility** — Electrical Safety First lists Type I plugs, 230 V, and 50 Hz. Check each device's input label and the actual outlet. A plug adapter changes fit, not voltage. A 120 V-only appliance needs manufacturer-approved conversion or should be left at home.

Checked: 2026-11-05.

Original section sources: [Electrical Safety First — Australia: travel adaptors](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/safety-advice/travel-advice/australia/) (checked 2026-11-05)

### Vaccines

**Health preparation for your itinerary** — Avoid mosquito bites. CDC identifies Japanese-encephalitis concerns in the Murray River and Outer Torres Strait Islands areas; vaccine advice depends on route, activities, and duration. Review routine vaccines, including measles protection. Any global CDC notice is not by itself a country-specific outbreak report. Arrange advice before travel; this summary does not prescribe treatment.

Checked: 2026-11-05.

Original section sources: [U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention — Australia: Traveler View](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/australia) (checked 2026-11-05)

### National and regional trains (JR trains in design)

**Allow for long distances and local transport systems** — Australian cities and regional destinations can be far apart. Depending on the route, travel may involve flights, trains, coaches, ferries, or local public transport. Check the local operator’s payment and service information instead of expecting one pass to work nationwide. For Sydney and the surrounding Opal network, the official Opal Travel app helps with journey information and card management; it is a regional tool.

Checked: 2026-11-05.

Original section sources: [Australian Government myGov — How to travel around Australia](https://my.gov.au/en/services/living-arrangements/coming-to-australia/coming-to-australia-to-visit/tips-when-you-visit/how-to-travel-around-australia) (checked 2026-11-05); [Transport for NSW — Opal Travel app](https://transportnsw.info/plan/transport-apps/opal-travel-app) (checked 2026-11-05)

## Local culture

### Etiquette

**Follow the local host’s cultural guidance** — Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities have different cultural protocols. Ask your host or guide before photographing people, artworks, or cultural sites, and respect places that are closed to visitors. Do not assume that permission at one place applies somewhere else. A locally led experience can help explain the protocols for that community; treat the host’s instructions as specific to that visit.

Checked: 2026-11-05.

Original section sources: [Discover Aboriginal Experiences / Tourism Australia collective — Understanding Aboriginal etiquette](https://www.discoveraboriginalexperiences.com/article/understanding-aboriginal-etiquette) (checked 2026-11-05); [Creative Workplaces, Australian Government — Collaborating respectfully with First Nations creatives and communities](https://creativeworkplaces.gov.au/first-nations-relations/first-nations-collaborations/collaborating-respectfully-first-nations) (checked 2026-11-05)
