### Research Notes

**Supplied research note (unverified):** Checked 2026-10-14. • Advisory: UK Level 2, Exercise Increased Caution (terrorism), reissued Sep 9, 2026 (State Dept). The UK FCDO doesn't publish advice for the UK itself. • Entry: U.S. citizens need a UK ETA before travel (enforced since Feb 25, 2026). £20, valid 2 years or until the passport expires, visits up to 6 months; allow 3 working days. Arriving via Ireland doesn't exempt you unless you live there (GOV.UK). • Passport valid for the whole stay, 1 blank page per entry stamp. Declare cash of £10,000 or more (State Dept). • Money: pound sterling (GBP). Three Scottish banks print their own notes; they're backed but not legal tender, so shops can refuse them (Bank of England). Tipping about 10% (VisitScotland). • Edinburgh visitor levy: 5% on overnight stays, up to 5 nights, since Jul 24, 2026. Glasgow's levy starts Jan 25, 2027 (city councils). • Power: Type G plugs, 230 V. U.S. plugs need an adapter; 120 V-only devices need a converter (VisitScotland). • Emergency: 999 or 112; 101 for non-emergency police; NHS 24 on 111. U.S. Consulate General Edinburgh +44-131-556-8315; after hours via Embassy London +44-20-7499-9000 (Police Scotland, State Dept). • Health: NHS emergency treatment is free only until you're stabilized; after that visitors pay, so buy travel insurance with medical cover (NHS inform). State Dept quotes England's 150% charge; Scotland's rate is unconfirmed. • Scots law: the drink-drive limit is about 0.05 BAC, lower than England's 0.08, so even one drink can count (State Dept). • Outdoors: access rights come with the Scottish Outdoor Access Code. In a hill emergency, dial 999, ask for Police, then Mountain Rescue (NatureScot, Scottish Mountain Rescue). • Source problems: no working ScotRail fares page verified; the U.S. Embassy site blocks bots; Ofcom's coverage API needs a subscription key; use TNDS TransXChange data for buses (it excludes rail). • Hilna's England column shares the UK-wide entry, money and emergency rules.
