# RoamReady information collection guide

## Who the information is for

RoamReady helps travelers using a **U.S. passport** plan trips to whole countries, for trips that may last days, weeks, months, or years. The planner can record exact start and return dates, or an estimated stay length (optionally with a start date). It also records the trip purpose: tourism, study, work, visiting family or friends, long-term stay or relocation, or transit.

These answers are planning context, not proof that a traveler qualifies for entry or permission to work or study. Do not collect passport numbers, health details, or precise location for this prototype.

## Country guide content and display standard

Every country guide must be researched for a traveler using a **U.S. passport** and written so a traveler with limited English vocabulary can understand it. The guide covers the whole country, not only its capital or tourist cities. City, airport, region, or provider details may be added when they are clearly labeled as local examples.

Do not assume that “under 90 days” means visa-free, or that every country's long-stay rules begin at 90 days. Show the two planning choices **Up to 90 days** and **More than 90 days**, but state each country's real allowed stay and the threshold used by its own rules. A fact must match the traveler’s passport, trip purpose, exact or estimated length, and travel dates where relevant. If a source cannot answer that case, say what is unknown and link the traveler to the responsible authority.

### Required information by topic

| Topic | Research and display requirements |
| --- | --- |
| **Entry and documents** | Give U.S.-passport rules for stays up to 90 days and more than 90 days. State visa or visa-free conditions, the maximum stay, purpose limits, passport validity, onward/return ticket rules, permitted extensions if any, and requirements for work, study, family visits, transit, and residence when the official source covers them. Do not imply that a short-stay tourist rule permits work, study, or an extension. Link official immigration/embassy pages and show when checked. |
| **Getting there** | Explain the country-level arrival steps and what to prepare. Check official border, customs, and airport sources for new forms, electronic travel authorizations, biometrics, pre-registration, health checks, baggage/customs rules, and other border-control changes. Call out a change only when its start date and who it applies to are clear. Label any airport-specific step. |
| **Getting around** | Give a countrywide overview of trains, buses, metro, ferries, domestic flights, taxis, and driving as relevant. Recommend a small number of genuinely useful, currently available apps for the country's main transport modes. Each app needs an official app-store or operator link, supported platform/region, what it helps with, and a checked date. Do not imply one city app works nationwide. State when routes, access, fares, or coverage depend on local operators. |
| **Money and payments** | Name the local currency and show an **USD-to-local-currency** conversion because the intended user is traveling from the United States. Use a current, timestamped source; identify the rate as an estimate and explain that card issuers, cash exchanges, and ATMs can apply different fees and rates. Do not show an undated rate as current. Cover card/ATM/cash patterns, U.S. card issues, tipping only when a reliable source supports it, and customs cash declaration thresholds with the correct direction, covered items, and effective date. |
| **Safety, weather, and disruptions** | Include current U.S. travel advisory information and relevant local safety alerts. Use the traveler's entered dates to identify known seasonal hazards and current disruptions. Explain the risk window in plain language (for example, which months are usually affected) and practical steps to take. If trip dates fall within an active or higher-risk season, put that warning near the top. If exact-date forecasts are available, show the forecast separately from long-term climate patterns, label the forecast dates and source, and avoid presenting a climate average as a forecast. If dates are too far away for a forecast, show only seasonal patterns and say that conditions can change. If there is no known seasonal hazard for the selected dates, show general expected weather for that time of year, not a fabricated danger warning. |
| **Health, care, and medicine** | Write a short, easy-to-read warning and next steps based on current CDC destination guidance, CDC notices, and the destination's health/medicine authorities. Cover relevant vaccines and health notices, common destination risks, when advice depends on trip length, season, rural/outdoor activities or personal health, how to prepare for care/insurance, and medicine import restrictions. Clearly label global notices as global. Do not diagnose, prescribe, or tell an individual that a medicine is legal based only on its brand name. Link to CDC and the official local authority; advise consulting a clinician when guidance depends on the person. |
| **Local culture** | Include widely useful customs and less widely known, practical details that help avoid confusion or offense. Separate **law**, **venue rule**, and **common custom** so a visitor can tell them apart. Prefer concrete, kind instructions; avoid stereotypes or claims about how every resident behaves. Provide a source link for each factual detail, especially anything new that was not in the supplied research. |
| **Phones and internet** | Give a few current eSIM or SIM options that work in the country. Verify availability to U.S. travelers, supported devices, coverage, data/voice limits, activation requirements, hotspot restrictions, and official purchase/support links. Explain that the phone must be compatible and, where required, unlocked. Compare with U.S. carrier roaming without claiming that one provider is “best” without evidence. Keep provider prices and packages dated because they change often. |
| **Electricity and tech** | State plug shape, voltage, and frequency, then explain in plain language whether the traveler likely needs a plug adapter, voltage converter, or neither. Tell the traveler to check the input label on each device; a plug adapter does not change voltage. If linking to a retailer such as Amazon, link to a specific compatible product only after checking its specifications, distinguish it from a voltage converter, date the check, and disclose any affiliate relationship. Do not link to a generic “universal converter” without confirming the device and country's requirements. |
| **Emergency information** | Keep verified local police, fire, and ambulance numbers prominent, with any country-specific dialing or language tips. Link to current U.S. embassy/consulate help for U.S. citizens and explain its limits. Recheck numbers and contact links before publication and on a recurring schedule. |

### Plain-language display rules

- Use common words, short sentences, clear headings, and direct actions (“Check your passport date”) instead of abstract labels (“passport validity”). Explain terms such as “visa,” “transit,” “exchange rate,” and “voltage converter” the first time they appear.
- Put the most important action or warning first. Use bullets for steps, and separate what is required from what is recommended.
- Show who the information applies to, how long it was checked to be current, and a direct source link. Show effective dates and time zones where they matter.
- Keep conditional advice visibly conditional: “If you plan to work…” / “If your trip is in July…” / “If your stay is longer than the allowed short visit…”
- Keep source language and internal research notes out of traveler-facing copy. Rewrite them in simple words without changing the meaning or dropping exceptions.

### Date-aware and frequently changing information

The trip form's dates should affect what the app shows, not just be stored. Use exact dates when supplied; if the user gives only a stay length, explain that date-based weather and seasonal warnings need a start date. Separate:

1. **Rules and facts** from official sources, each with checked/effective dates.
2. **Seasonal patterns** based on a named climate or hazard source and a defined month/region.
3. **Weather forecasts and live disruptions** from a forecast or alert service, labeled with issue time, valid period, geographic area, and provider.
4. **Live estimates** such as USD exchange rates, labeled with currency pair, rate source, retrieval time, and a note that the traveler's bank or exchange provider may charge differently.

Set review intervals by how quickly the information changes. Entry rules, border controls, travel alerts, health notices, forecasts, exchange rates, provider prices, and timetables need live checks or frequent refreshes. An unavailable source must never be treated as proof that no alert or rule exists. If dynamic information cannot be refreshed, show its last-checked time and tell the user to confirm it with the source.

## What the research bank gives us

The four country CSVs cover 24 countries and use ten topic areas: entry and documents; getting there; getting around; money and payments; safety; health; local culture; phones and internet; electricity and tech; emergency information. They provide research leads and early notes. Cells mix links, source names, claims, and commentary, so they are not clean records and should not be published directly.

Preserve country-level coverage and the country slugs in the existing catalog. For every candidate, record the country and topic, source page, publisher, jurisdiction, checked date, and the specific claim or question the source may answer. A source listed in a CSV is a lead to inspect, not evidence that each nearby statement is current.

## Collect and publish in two stages

1. **Candidate collection:** Fetch only permitted, public pages from an allowlist of official or otherwise suitable publishers. Save page title, canonical page URL, publisher, page jurisdiction, retrieval time, content hash, and a short excerpt or change summary. Keep raw output private and mark it `needs_review`.
2. **Human review and publication:** A reviewer opens the source, checks that it still supports the claim, confirms that it applies to U.S. passport holders and the stated trip purpose/stay, rewrites the point in plain language, and records reviewer and verification date. Publish only after this check. Link users to the source page.

For changes, create a new candidate version and compare it with the prior version. A page update is a review alert; it does not automatically change traveler guidance. Keep the previous verified version available until a replacement has passed review.

## Use sources with the right authority

- **Entry, visa, residence, work and study permission:** the destination country's immigration authority, embassy, consulate, or official legal/gazette source. Confirm the rule for U.S. citizens and the specific purpose and duration where the authority distinguishes them.
- **Safety and U.S. government help:** U.S. Department of State country pages, travel advisories, and embassy/consulate pages.
- **Health:** U.S. CDC travel health notices and destination health authorities. Clearly date time-sensitive notices.
- **Arrival and transport:** official airport, border, railway, transit authority, and government transport pages.
- **Money and customs:** central bank, customs authority, and official financial regulators. Clearly label rates and limits with their effective or checked dates.
- **Culture, connectivity, and electricity:** official tourism or telecom/utility sources for practical context; corroborate rules or safety claims with the responsible authority.

The CSV research notes include UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office material. It can be useful as a secondary safety perspective, but it cannot establish entry rules for a U.S.-passport user. The current collector's FCDO pages are UK-jurisdiction sources; keep their output as candidates and do not present it as U.S.-specific advice.

## What each published fact needs

Before a fact can appear in the app, store:

- destination slug and one of the ten topic IDs;
- audience/jurisdiction (`US passport holder`) and, when relevant, purpose and stay-length conditions stated by the source;
- a short title and clear explanation, including exceptions or next steps that change what the traveler should do;
- source page title, publisher, canonical URL, source jurisdiction, and checked date;
- source publication/effective date when available, plus `last_verified_at`, reviewer, and publication status;
- a review-by date or volatility label for facts that can change quickly (entry requirements, advisories, health notices, fares, and limits).

Published facts and unreviewed candidates now have `min_stay_days`, `max_stay_days`, `applies_to_purposes`, and `applicability_status`. Day limits are inclusive. A null purpose list means any purpose; null day limits mean no limit. A fact marked `general` has no conditions; `conditional` requires at least one condition; `needs_review` must never be shown to travelers. Candidate rows begin as `needs_review` and only become eligible after a reviewer checks the conditions as well as the claim. The facts API can take an estimated day range and purpose, then includes unrestricted facts plus facts whose conditions overlap that trip. If a traveler gives no duration or purpose, conditional facts are hidden rather than guessed.

The schema still needs audience/jurisdiction, region, valid-from/valid-until dates, source checked time, and volatility/review interval before the product can safely personalize every category. Live forecast and currency values must come from date-aware services at display time rather than be copied into permanent country facts.

Never infer a universal visa-free stay, work permission, or extension rule from a destination summary. Explain when an answer depends on nationality, purpose, length, transit, or other conditions, and send the traveler to the responsible authority when the source does not resolve their case. Do not turn uncertain or conflicting material into definitive guidance.

## Collection rules

- Respect each publisher's terms, robots rules, rate limits, attribution requirements, and data license. Prefer official APIs, feeds, or change notices where available.
- Stop when access is denied or automation is disallowed. Do not bypass login, CAPTCHA, bot checks, paywalls, or technical controls; ask for an official feed or use manual review instead.
- Keep source text and collector output out of the public frontend. Avoid storing more copied text than the team needs to review and document the source/license.
- Store failed fetches and blocked sources as operational status only; do not treat failure or a missing page as evidence that a rule changed.
- Show users the checked date and direct source link. If a high-impact fact is overdue or its source is unavailable, mark it for review or hide it until checked.

## Prototype implementation notes

The facts API accepts `stay_days_min`, `stay_days_max`, and `purpose`; for example, a short tourism query can use `stay_days_min=1&stay_days_max=90&purpose=tourism`, and a long work query can use `stay_days_min=91&stay_days_max=365&purpose=work`. The planner stores an exact day count for entered dates and a possible day range for estimated months/years. Estimated ranges that cross a fact's boundary include that fact as possibly relevant. The current frontend still uses content placeholders and has not yet connected Supabase sign-in or sent its saved trip profile to the protected facts API, so end-to-end personalized display is not complete.

The existing collector writes source candidates for review; there are currently no published travel facts. Start with one country and a small set of high-impact topics (entry, safety, health, and emergency help), validate the source/review workflow, then expand country coverage. A successful scraper run means only that a candidate was collected, not that the app is ready to advise a traveler.
